# web-game-factory 2.7.0: games that are designed, not just built

Released 2026-10-03. Template work: none — the pin stays at v1.2.0 (`b106261`).

## Why this release exists

2.6.0 made the Factory play, measure and look at the running build. What it still could not
say was **whether the build was a build of a game**. A design could pass every check and
describe one level and a difficulty ramp; a developer then invented the levels, the win
condition and the curve around it, and reported the result as a prototype of that design. G4
was asked to kill or pass a game nobody had written down.

Three causes, each where it belongs:

- **`core/` held no genre knowledge.** Nothing said what a game of a genre is made of, so
  there was no shape for a design to be held to and no bar a one-level prototype could fail.
- **The design artifact held no content.** The only list of what a player meets was the
  difficulty curve, which is a shape, not a list of levels, waves, tracks or encounters.
- **A developer had no way to say "the design does not decide this."** So it decided, and the
  decision reached the gate looking like the designer's.

2.7.0 makes the content a contract: stated in the design, planned as tasks, built as data,
played from outside, and returned to the designer when it was not stated.

It also carries **Research V2**, which makes the game the unit of research: a coded corpus,
market cells that keep demand and supply apart, counted patterns, and five generators that
propose several opportunities instead of screening a fixed catalog of eleven shapes
([research-v2.md](research-v2.md)).

## What changed

**Genre models as data** — `core/reference/genre-models.yaml` 1.0.0. Eight families (puzzle,
platformer, arcade, shooter, racing, strategy, survival, simulation), each stating the genre
nodes it covers, the unit kinds, progression models, difficulty models and endings that fit it,
the difficulty axes (which escalate, which the build must report), the shape of win and loss,
the units an MVP and a release carry, the variety dimensions, the mastery model, the QA
parameters the playability checks read, and a `seed` block a complete design is synthesized
from. `casual` and `standard` are session profiles, not families. Nothing branches on a family
id — a new family is an entry, never a code path. Craft:
`core/craft/content-and-level-design.md`; skill: `level-design` (adapter binding 1.7.0).

**The design states its content** — `game-design` 1.9.0: `genre`, `build_spec.content`
(unit kind, generation mode, every unit with purpose, objective, mechanics, per-axis difficulty,
duration, success, failure, acceptance, variation), `build_spec.difficulty.axes`,
`build_spec.mastery`, `consistency.content_model`, and a win required of a `finite` game. The
design step holds it to the family (22 `content.*` rules) after the depth check. Strategy
resolves the shape first (`title-strategy` 1.4.0 `concept.content_model`), from research's
`design_constraints` or the family's default.

**The build is held to it** — the developer brief carries the genre, the content table, the
axes, mastery and depth; an authored design owes `public/content/units.json` and
`tests/unit/content.test.ts`, compared with the design by `conformance`; the tech plan makes one
`CONTENT-nnn` task per batch of three MVP units; the playability bot traverses the units and
measures difficulty, persistence and session length (eight new checks, all bars in data);
`verify` carries that evidence as `quality.*` and the `gameplay-quality` suite rather than
re-measuring it; review reads a `## Design fidelity` section; G3 and G4 are shown the content
rules, the coverage, the gaps and the played checks. A `SKIPPED` check is never a pass.

**A return route** — workflow 8: `design-gap` from `greybox` and `develop` back to `design`,
one pass from each, with the `prototype-report` whose `design_gaps` name what the design did not
decide. The build is kept; tech-plan and G3 run again on the repaired design.

**Research V2** — the game corpus and vocabulary, teardown records, market cells, counted
patterns and benchmarks, five opportunity generators, capability gaps, the backlog, and the
research handoff strategy and design read. Full account: [research-v2.md](research-v2.md).

**Buildability has two roads** — capability catalog 1.3.0: an entry is buildable through a
`design_archetype` or a `genre_model`. Eight more shapes are buildable with no rule relaxed.

Schema changes, all additive — every earlier artifact remains valid: `game-design` 1.9.0,
`title-strategy` 1.4.0, `opportunity` 1.3.0, `research-report` 1.2.0, `prototype-report` 1.1.0,
`playability-report` 1.2.0, `qa-report` 1.2.0, `review-report` 1.1.0, and the shared
`play-probe` and `research-opportunity` fields. Reference data: `genre-models.yaml` 1.0.0,
`design-consistency-rules.yaml` 1.3.0, `design-depth.yaml` 1.1.0, capability catalog 1.3.0,
adapter binding 1.7.0.

## Upgrading from 2.6.x

**Nothing to configure.** Every new block is optional in its schema, and a design written
against 1.8.0 keeps validating.

**A legacy design gets none of this.** A design with no `genre` resolves to no family: the
content check records one warning, applies no bar, and the build is held to no content contract.
To bring a title forward, re-run the design step — which re-resolves the family from the
strategy's `concept.content_model`, the research, the genre node or the concept's words.

**Projects with a project `workspace/config/factory.yaml`** need no change. The autonomous
profile already auto-approves G2 and G3, which is what lets a design repair finish unattended;
a supervised installation stops once per `design-gap` return for the G3 decision, by design.

**Installations with their own capability catalog** may now name `genre_model` on an entry
instead of a `design_archetype`. An entry that declares neither field still makes no claim.

## Not covered

- **G4, G6 and G7 stay human.** A `/new-game` run still stops at G4.
- **Only the agent author designs an idea.** A catalog entry that names a genre family is a
  capability, not a game: the strategy then carries the person's idea as the concept, and the
  deterministic seed - one particular game per family - refuses it by name. With the shipped
  configuration `wgf new-game "IDEA"` for one of the eight new families reaches design and
  stops there; a catalog-led run (no idea) is designed offline.
- **Seven of the eight families have no live build.** They are validated offline and in the
  suite. The one live run (`ice-slide`) stopped at the production art gate with placeholder
  fonts and audio, so no content-bearing design has been through visual-QA, review, verify or
  G4.
- **No golden run covers authored content.** Both golden designs are parametric arcade, so the
  data-file comparison and the unit traversal checks skip there. A level game is needed as a
  port before that path has a regression run.
- **The genre bars are not calibrated from shipped titles.** `units.min_mvp`, the variety shares
  and the session multipliers are defensible defaults, not measurements. Playtests and live data
  are what move them, and moving one is a change to a versioned file with a stated reason.
- **No convention is counted yet.** `research.design_constraints.conventions` needs teardown
  records; the shipped corpus has none, so nothing reaches the prevalence bar.
- **Mastery and variety are judged structurally, not by a player.** A bot can see that
  consecutive units differ and that a signal is on screen; whether getting better feels like
  getting better is a stranger playtest and G4.
